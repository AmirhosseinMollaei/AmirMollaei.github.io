**Status: under review at WACV 2027.**

Choosing informative camera views matters for training and refining a 3D Gaussian Splatting model,
because each observation moves the parameters a lot. The cost is that information-driven selection
keeps calling an expensive information-gain oracle, and the number of calls grows with the candidate
pool.

LiTe-GS evaluates a randomized subset of candidates rather than scoring the pool exhaustively. The
result is expected `O(M log(1/e))` oracle complexity in the number of candidate views `M`,
independent of how many views are finally selected, with the parameter `e` giving an explicit dial
between oracle efficiency and approximation quality. The paper proves bounds on both the oracle
complexity and the approximation performance of the scheme.

On Blender and Mip-NeRF 360, reconstruction quality stays comparable to Fisher-information
baselines while the number of Fisher-oracle evaluations drops substantially across acquisition
settings.
